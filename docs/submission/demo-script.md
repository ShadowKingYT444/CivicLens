# CivicLens Congressional App Challenge demonstration

Target length: **2 minutes 50 seconds**. Record a working app, with narration and readable screen capture. The official 2026 rules require a public 1–3 minute video identifying every participant, the app, purpose, audience, tools/languages, and functioning features. [Official rules, section 4](https://www.congressionalappchallenge.us/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/2026-CAC-Rules.pdf).

## Recording setup

- Replace the participant-name placeholder with every registered team member's full name. Do not invent a team or claim other people's work as your own.
- Start the app using the repository's setup instructions. This review verified real NVIDIA NIM analysis with the supplied server key; rehearse the exact inputs again on the recording device. Choose live-AI narration only when that response succeeds with live labels. If using `pnpm demo`, use the guided/fallback narration instead. `/api/health` key detection alone is insufficient.
- Record a clean browser at a readable phone or tablet width. Close notifications, developer tools, account pages, and anything displaying secrets. Use a public building address, never a student's home address.
- Use `H.R. 82` from the **118th Congress** throughout this demo: bill numbers repeat across Congresses. The fixture describes an enacted historical law; do not introduce it as a new bill.
- Keep mode labels visible. Fixtures and deterministic explanations are demonstration data, not proof that a live provider worked. If AI is configured, record the successful live response. If it is unavailable, use the alternate sentence below.
- Keep the final video under three minutes including titles and credits. Aim for 2:50 to leave a margin. Upload to **public** YouTube or Vimeo, and test the HTTPS link in a signed-out browser.

## Script and shot list

### 0:00–0:20 — Purpose and audience

**Screen:** Homepage, then tap Learn. Show the app name immediately.

**Say:**

“I'm [your full name; introduce every teammate]. This is CivicLens: an app that helps students turn political claims into source-backed civic understanding. It is designed for middle-school and high-school students who need to understand what government can do, what a bill actually says, and how to check the evidence.”

### 0:20–0:50 — Learn by doing

**Screen:** Open the first available lesson from the learning path. Advance through its three flashcards, complete its three-question quiz, and show the explanation and updated path. You may trim quiet reading time while preserving the actual interactions and feedback.

**Say:**

“The learning path has 32 short lessons and 96 application questions, from government foundations to civic practice. A question tests the concept rather than political agreement. Feedback explains the answer, and completing a lesson updates progress. The goal is to make government understandable through concrete steps that students can apply.”

### 0:50–1:30 — Analyze and inspect evidence

**Screen:** Open Analyze. Enter **“What did H.R. 82 of the 118th Congress change about Social Security?”** Submit, show the answer and evidence label, and open a cited source. If generation takes time, trim the wait while keeping the final mode label visible; do not substitute a fabricated response.

**Say:**

“Here I ask what H.R. 82 changed. The historical sources explain that it repealed two rules that could reduce benefits for some pension recipients. CivicLens retrieves source material and turns it into a plain-English explanation. I can open the source to check it. Citation checks reduce unsupported output; they do not make AI infallible. Missing evidence must stay visible.”

**Fact reference for rehearsal:** [Social Security Administration legislative bulletin](https://www.ssa.gov/legislation/legis_bulletin_010624.html) confirms the January 5, 2025 enactment and repeal of WEP/GPO. This is the factual example, not a promise of any individual's benefit amount.

**Mode sentence — choose one:**

- Successful live response: “This response was generated by the configured AI provider using the retrieved sources.”
- Fixture/fallback response: “This recording uses labeled demonstration data and a deterministic explanation; the live AI connection is configured separately.”

### 1:30–1:55 — Follow a bill's record

**Screen:** Open `/bills/118/hr/82`. Show title, action timeline, and Sources. Do not describe illustrative vote graphics as actual roll-call results.

**Say:**

“The bill explorer connects the explanation to its official record: the Congress, summary, sponsors, actions, and source links. Here, a historical bill's timeline shows its progression into law. Keeping those identifiers visible matters because the same bill number can refer to different legislation in different Congresses.”

### 1:55–2:15 — Local connection and privacy

**Screen:** Open District and tap **“Explore a sample district.”** Show its California District 11 example, the **July 6, 2026 snapshot** notice, source controls, and privacy explanation. Use this explicit sample flow for the short recording. Real address lookup is a separate flow; without a Congress API key, the app can show a verified Census district and official directory links rather than claiming current member names were fetched.

**Say:**

“District lookup connects civic learning to federal representation. This is an explicitly dated sample snapshot, not a live member lookup. Real addresses are processed on the server and are not stored or sent to the language model. The app keeps example data separate from verified district information.”

### 2:15–2:50 — Engineering, AI disclosure, and close

**Screen:** Show Methodology, then return to the learning path or homepage. Add a small tools caption rather than opening environment files.

**Say:**

“CivicLens uses TypeScript and JavaScript, Next.js, React, Tailwind CSS, Zod, and optional PostgreSQL with Prisma. Congress.gov and Census provide civic data, with configurable AI providers. Codex and coordinated AI agents assisted implementation, curriculum, and testing; that assistance is disclosed in the submission. The app declines campaign persuasion and voting recommendations. The aim is to help students understand an issue, inspect its evidence, and explain it for themselves.”

## Rehearsal checks

- Every registered participant is named; purpose and student audience are clear.
- The lesson, answer explanation, and progress shown are real interactions.
- Analysis succeeds for the exact input; sources open; live/fallback status is accurately narrated.
- The historical bill route opens and its timeline/source controls work.
- The District sample notice and snapshot date remain visible; no example representative is presented as a live verified result.
- No production claims imply citations guarantee correctness, demonstrated adoption, or measured learning gains.
- Video duration is 1–3 minutes; the published video is public and reachable without sign-in.

## Verified behavior in the prepared build

Agent-run Playwright checks passed the exact H.R. 82 question, fallback disclosure, evidence excerpts, cited bill link, and analysis practice quiz. The first lesson's three flashcards and three questions passed, including retry after an incorrect answer, completion, saved progress, and replay without duplicated rewards. The District sample disclosure, source modal keyboard controls, and phone-to-desktop layouts were also checked. These are engineering checks, not measurements of student learning or adoption. A live LLM response remains unverified because no provider credentials were supplied.

The narrator should understand and be able to explain the source retrieval, validation, lesson progress, privacy, and fallback logic. The 2026 rules require significant student contributions and technical understanding even when AI assistance is disclosed.
